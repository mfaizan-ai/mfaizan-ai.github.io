I am a PhD candidate in Psychology at [Trinity College Dublin](https://www.tcd.ie), working in the [Cusack Lab](https://www.cusacklab.org/home.html) under the supervision of [Prof. Rhodri Cusack](https://www.tcd.ie/research/profiles/?profile=cusackrh). My research focuses on developing physics-informed deep learning frameworks that integrate MRI acquisition physics with data-driven learning to improve image reconstruction and quality. By leveraging raw k-space measurements and complementary information from multiple receiver coils, I aim to enhance reconstruction fidelity and address challenges associated with motion in infant neuroimaging. Our reconstruction pipeline achieved 64% lower slice leakage and 26% lower ghosting compared with the Siemens online reconstruction, while maintaining broadly comparable image quality and demonstrating favourable noise-amplification characteristics.

Building on this work, I develop generative deep learning methods for correcting motion artefacts in fMRI by learning spatial and temporal dependencies while incorporating domain-specific knowledge of blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) signals and motion-induced distortions. By formulating motion correction as an unpaired image-to-image translation problem, my work aims to suppress motion-related artefacts while preserving meaningful neural activity and functional connectivity. Compared with the original uncorrected data, our approach reduced significant motion-related functional connectivity associations by 99.8% and distance-dependent motion bias by 86.9%, while increasing functional network modularity by 114.8% and inter-subject correlation by 44.8%, demonstrating substantial reductions in motion-related effects alongside improvements in measures of functional organisation and shared brain responses.

Before beginning my PhD, I completed my Master’s in Robotics and Intelligent Machine Engineering at the [National University of Sciences and Technology](https://nust.edu.pk) (NUST), Islamabad, Pakistan. My Master’s research focused on developing a hybrid deep neural network architecture for brain tumor segmentation using multi-modal structural brain MRI. This work was supervised by [Dr. Sara Baber Sial](https://research.monash.edu/en/persons/sara-sial/).
